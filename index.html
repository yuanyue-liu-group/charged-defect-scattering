Texas Materials Institute and Department of Mechanical Engineering,
The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, Texas 78712, USA

Abstract

Charged defect scattering of electrons plays a critical role in determining a wide range of material properties. However, there is a lack of an accurate method to calculate the scattering, as all the existing methods require assuming a specific distribution for unscreened (i.e., bare) excess charge carried by the defect, which limits their accuracy. Here we develop a first-principles method to determine the bare charge distribution, which thus enables accurate calculations of the scattering. Using this method, we accurately calculate the electron mobility of 2D MoS2 under the scattering of charged sulfur vacancies and compare with that of charge-neutral oxygen substitutes of sulfur. Interestingly, we find that the charged defect-limited mobility exhibits a strong temperature dependence at low temperature and carrier concentration, in contrast with the neutral defect and the conventional belief that the defect-limited mobility is less sensitive to the temperature. This behavior arises from the free carrier screening of the long-range scattering potential of the charged defect.

Method

The quantity to compute is the scattering matrix element Mn,n′(k,k′) = ⟨n′k′|ΔV|nk⟩. Its difficulty is that ΔV for a charged defect has a long-range Coulomb component, requiring a supercell far too large for direct DFT. The formation of the charged defect is therefore decomposed into two steps — a perfect structure (PS) becoming a neutral defective structure (NS) at the charged-defect geometry, and that structure then becoming charged (CS):

(2–4) ΔV = ΔVPS→NS + ΔVNS→CS,    ΔVNS→CS = ΔVXCNS→CS + ΔVHNS→CS ΔVPS→NS and ΔVXCNS→CS are short ranged and converge quickly with supercell size, so both come from modest DFT supercells. Only the Hartree term ΔVHNS→CS is long ranged.

The long-range term is obtained from the bare excess charge Δρb and the material's screening, rather than from a large supercell. The central result is that Δρb need not be assumed — it follows from the total density change Δρtot of a small supercell together with the bound-electron polarizability χ0b of the primitive cell:

(11) · descreening Δρb = Δρtot − χ0b v ( Δρtot − Q/Ω ) Q is the defect charge, Ω the supercell volume, and Q/Ω the jellium background that compensates it in the DFT calculation. Unlike the long-ranged ΔVHNS→CS, Δρb is localized around the defect — which is exactly why a small supercell suffices.
(5–6) · rescreening ΔVHNS→CS = W Δρb,    W = ( I − vχ0 )−1 v,    χ0 = χ0b + χ0c Because Δρb is localized, it can be transferred to an arbitrarily large supercell and rescreened there. Free carriers enter through χ0c, computed from a modified Adler–Wiser formula rather than a Thomas–Fermi or Lindhard model — this is what makes the result depend on temperature and carrier concentration.

Results for 2D MoS2

The method is applied to the two dominant defects in 2D MoS2: the −1 charged sulfur vacancy (VS−1) and the charge-neutral oxygen substitute of sulfur (OS). Mobilities are computed separately for each across carrier concentrations and temperatures.

T−0.35

Below 150 K at 1×1011 cm−2, mobility under VS−1 scattering falls with temperature, while neutral OS leaves it nearly unchanged.

< 0.45×

To limit mobility as much as OS does, the concentration of VS−1 needs to be below 0.45 times that of OS.

1013

Mobility under VS−1 first rises with carrier density, peaking near 1×1013 cm−2 as free carriers screen the defect.

The strong temperature dependence of charged-defect-limited mobility is unexpected: higher temperature weakens χ0c and therefore the screening, strengthening the scattering. Neutral OS has no ΔVHNS→CS component and so is insensitive to free-carrier screening. Combining the calculated defect- and phonon-limited mobilities by Matthiessen's rule reproduces measured mobilities of 2D MoS2, with fitted defect concentrations consistent with reported values.

Workflow

The full calculation runs in nine stages. Stage 5 is an independent branch that must be repeated for every temperature and carrier concentration.

  1. Ground-state DFT

    Primitive cell for bands and wavefunctions; three supercells — perfect (PS), neutral defective (NS), and charged (CS) — for the potential and density changes.

    out ΔVPS→NS, ΔVXCNS→CS, Δρtot  ·  code Quantum ESPRESSO

  2. Bound-electron polarizability

    χ0b from the primitive cell on a q-grid matching the supercell being treated, with nonuniform neck subsampling near q = 0.

    out chimat.h5, chi0mat.h5  ·  code BerkeleyGW

  3. Descreening

    Apply Eq. (11) to recover the bare charge from the small-supercell density change.

    out Δρb  ·  code potcorr.PotCorr

  4. Transfer to a large supercell

    Inverse-FFT Δρb to real space, zero the extended region, forward-FFT on the larger cell. Valid because Δρb is localized.

    code utli.rho_expand

  5. Free-carrier polarizability

    χ0c from a modified Adler–Wiser formula, factorized into an out-of-plane profile ξ(z) and an in-plane response, with the Fermi level solved from the target carrier concentration and temperature.

    out χ0c(q∥) per (T, ne), ξ(z)

  6. Rescreening

    Build W = (I − vχ0)−1v and apply Eq. (5). For the 2D material the out-of-plane direction is treated in real space while the in-plane directions stay in reciprocal space, to respect the open boundary and finite thickness.

    out ΔVHNS→CS(q∥, z)

  7. Interpolate to the transport grid

    Radial basis function interpolation lifts the perturbation potential onto the dense k-mesh needed for mobility convergence.

    code potk2rbf.py

  8. Scattering matrix

    Combine the three potential contributions with primitive-cell wavefunctions to form Mn,n′(k,k′).

    out scattering matrix elements  ·  code calcmat.py

  9. Mobility

    Momentum relaxation times from |M|2 with a triangle integration of the energy delta, then the linearized Boltzmann transport equation.

    code EDI
