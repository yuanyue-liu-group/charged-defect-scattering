Accurate Ab Initio Method for Charged Defect Scattering

Rongjing Guo, Kwangrae Kim, Zhongcan Xiao, and Yuanyue Liu*

Texas Materials Institute and Department of Mechanical Engineering,
The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, Texas 78712, USA

doi:10.1103/42pc-t2bx EDI transport code Workflow Cite

Abstract

Charged defect scattering of electrons plays a critical role in determining a wide range of material properties. However, there is a lack of an accurate method to calculate the scattering, as all the existing methods require assuming a specific distribution for unscreened (i.e., bare) excess charge carried by the defect, which limits their accuracy. Here we develop a first-principles method to determine the bare charge distribution, which thus enables accurate calculations of the scattering. Using this method, we accurately calculate the electron mobility of 2D MoS2 under the scattering of charged sulfur vacancies and compare with that of charge-neutral oxygen substitutes of sulfur. Interestingly, we find that the charged defect-limited mobility exhibits a strong temperature dependence at low temperature and carrier concentration, in contrast with the neutral defect and the conventional belief that the defect-limited mobility is less sensitive to the temperature. This behavior arises from the free carrier screening of the long-range scattering potential of the charged defect.

Method

The quantity to compute is the scattering matrix element

𝑀𝑛,𝑛′(𝒌,𝒌′)=⟨𝑛′𝒌′|Δ𝑉|𝑛𝒌⟩
(1)

where |𝑛𝒌⟩ and |𝑛′𝒌′⟩ are the initial and final states. The difficulty is that Δ𝑉 for a charged defect has a long-range Coulomb component, requiring a supercell far too large for direct DFT.

The formation of the charged defect is therefore decomposed into two steps: a perfect structure (PS) becoming a neutral defective structure (NS) with the atoms at the charged-defect positions, and that structure then becoming charged (CS).

Δ𝑉=Δ𝑉PS→NS+Δ𝑉NS→CS,Δ𝑉NS→CS=Δ𝑉XCNS→CS+Δ𝑉HNS→CS
(2–4)

Δ𝑉PS→NS and Δ𝑉XCNS→CS are short ranged and converge quickly with supercell size, so both come from modest DFT supercells. Only the Hartree term Δ𝑉HNS→CS is long ranged.

That term is obtained from the bare excess charge Δ𝜌b together with the material's screening, rather than from a large supercell. The central result is that Δ𝜌b need not be assumed — it follows from the total density change Δ𝜌tot of a small supercell and the bound-electron polarizability 𝜒0b of the primitive cell:

Δ𝜌b=Δ𝜌tot−𝜒0b𝑣(Δ𝜌tot−𝑄/Ω)
(11)

𝑄 is the defect charge, Ω the supercell volume, and 𝑄/Ω the jellium background that compensates it in the DFT calculation. Unlike the long-ranged Δ𝑉HNS→CS, Δ𝜌b is localized around the defect — which is exactly why a small supercell suffices. This step is the descreening.

Δ𝑉HNS→CS=𝑊Δ𝜌b,𝑊=(𝐼−𝑣𝜒0)−1𝑣,𝜒0=𝜒0b+𝜒0c
(5–6)

Because Δ𝜌b is localized, it can be transferred to an arbitrarily large supercell and screened again there — the rescreening. Free carriers enter through 𝜒0c, computed from a modified Adler–Wiser formula rather than a Thomas–Fermi or Lindhard model, which is what makes the result depend on temperature and carrier concentration.

Results for 2D MoS2

The method is applied to the two dominant defects in 2D MoS2: the −1 charged sulfur vacancy (VS−1) and the charge-neutral oxygen substitute of sulfur (OS). Mobilities are computed separately for each across carrier concentrations and temperatures.

𝑇−0.35

Below 150 K at 1×1011 cm−2, mobility under VS−1 scattering falls with temperature, while neutral OS leaves it nearly unchanged.

<0.45×

To limit mobility as much as OS does, the concentration of VS−1 needs to be below 0.45 times that of OS.

1013 cm−2

Mobility under VS−1 first rises with carrier density, peaking near this concentration as free carriers screen the defect.

The strong temperature dependence of charged-defect-limited mobility is unexpected: higher temperature weakens 𝜒0c and therefore the screening, strengthening the scattering. Neutral OS has no Δ𝑉HNS→CS component and so is insensitive to free-carrier screening. Combining the calculated defect- and phonon-limited mobilities by Matthiessen's rule reproduces measured mobilities of 2D MoS2, with fitted defect concentrations consistent with reported values.

Workflow

The full calculation runs in nine stages. Stage 5 is an independent branch that must be repeated for every temperature and carrier concentration.

  1. Ground-state DFT

    Primitive cell for bands and wavefunctions; three supercells — perfect (PS), neutral defective (NS), and charged (CS) — for the potential and density changes.

    out Δ𝑉PS→NS, Δ𝑉XCNS→CS, Δ𝜌tot  ·  code Quantum ESPRESSO

  2. Bound-electron polarizability

    𝜒0b from the primitive cell on a q-grid matching the supercell being treated, with nonuniform neck subsampling near 𝑞 = 0.

    out chimat.h5, chi0mat.h5  ·  code BerkeleyGW

  3. Descreening

    Apply Eq. (11) to recover the bare charge from the small-supercell density change.

    out Δ𝜌b  ·  code potcorr

  4. Transfer to a large supercell

    Inverse-FFT Δ𝜌b to real space, zero the extended region, forward-FFT on the larger cell. Valid because Δ𝜌b is localized.

    code potcorr

  5. Free-carrier polarizability

    𝜒0c from a modified Adler–Wiser formula, factorized into an out-of-plane profile 𝜉(𝑧) and an in-plane response, with the Fermi level solved from the target carrier concentration and temperature.

    out 𝜒0c(𝑞∥) per (𝑇, 𝑛e), 𝜉(𝑧)

  6. Rescreening

    Build 𝑊 = (𝐼 − 𝑣𝜒0)−1𝑣 and apply Eq. (5). For the 2D material the out-of-plane direction is treated in real space while the in-plane directions stay in reciprocal space, to respect the open boundary and finite thickness.

    out Δ𝑉HNS→CS(𝑞∥, 𝑧)

  7. Interpolate to the transport grid

    Radial basis function interpolation lifts the perturbation potential onto the dense k-mesh needed for mobility convergence.

    code potcorr

  8. Scattering matrix

    Combine the three potential contributions with primitive-cell wavefunctions to form 𝑀𝑛,𝑛′(𝒌, 𝒌′).

    code potcorr

  9. Mobility

    Momentum relaxation times from |𝑀|2 with a triangle integration of the energy delta, then the linearized Boltzmann transport equation.

    code EDI
